# Boxabl Casita Roof Options — Engineering Guide

This page is a **summary** of the five roof option families shown in Boxabl's published Casita
presentation sets (sheets S5–S9, models BXB-000002 / BXB-000009). It is written for owners,
installers, and plan reviewers who want to understand what each option is and — critically —
**which parts of the roof are site-specific engineering that the factory documents do not cover.**

It is intentionally descriptive, not permit-ready.

## The One-Sentence Version

Boxabl's factory approval covers the box; the site-built roof over it is **on-site construction**
that your local building department permits separately — and Boxabl's own roof sheets repeatedly
say "SITE-SPECIFIC DESIGN CRITERIA TO BE PROVIDED BY A LICENSED ENGINEER."

## Important Boundary

The roof option sheets in Boxabl's presentation set are concept/reference drawings. The following
remain site-specific on every roof option and must be resolved for your parcel and jurisdiction:

- design wind speed, exposure category, ground snow load, and seismic design category
- truss design itself (by the truss manufacturer's engineer, to criteria a licensed engineer provides)
- truss-to-SIP-roof connection hardware and spacing (A34/A35 clips "spaced per engineer's specs")
- sheathing nailing schedules, which tighten in high wind (see below)
- orientation selection, which is mandated by seismic category (see below)
- any deviation from the drawn geometry ("designed by a structural engineer licensed in the state of installation")
- local code amendments and the permit submittal itself

## Shared Engineering Rules (from the truss option notes, S6–S8)

- Trusses are designed by the **truss manufacturer** assuming support only at the walls, and each
  truss transmits lateral wind from top chord to bottom chord.
- Bottom chords are restrained by A34 clips; eave connections use A35 clips **spaced per the
  engineer's specifications** — this spacing is a site-specific engineering output, not a default.
- Simpson Strong-Tie hardware per Simpson specifications unless noted.
- Connection info accommodating actual truss geometry is "to be designed by a structural engineer
  licensed in the state of installation and coordinated with the truss manufacturer's shop drawings."
- Bottom-chord bracing against uplift buckling: 2x6 between each truss, glued and fastened to the
  SIP roof with (3) #14 screws.
- If a truss lands on a SIP screw head, the truss bottom is shaved for flush contact.
- Roof pitches steeper than 5:12 require special design through Boxabl.
- Stability during erection is designed and supplied by others.

## Wind, Snow & Seismic Triggers Owners Miss

- **High wind:** if ultimate design wind speed exceeds **130 mph (Exposure B)** or **110 mph
  (Exposure C)**, sheathing edge and field nailing tightens to 4"oc within 4 ft of the perimeter.
  Much of the coastal South and plains country trips this rule.
- **Snow:** if ground snow load plus roofing weight exceeds **70 psf**, thicker rated sheathing is
  required. Mountain and northern lots trip this rule routinely.
- **Seismic orientation:** each truss option has two layout orientations. **Orientation #2 is
  required in Seismic Design Categories D and E** (D, E, and F on the hip option); Orientation #1
  is not allowed there. Practically: in most of California, western Nevada, the Wasatch Front,
  and western Washington/Oregon, the orientation is not a preference — it is dictated.
- **Roofing material:** clay or concrete tile is **not allowed** in Seismic Categories D/E (D, E,
  or F per the hip sheet). Tile-loving jurisdictions in high-seismic areas need an alternative.

## Why the State Pre-Approval Does Not Cover This

State modular programs approve what is built **in the factory** under the insignia. Site-installed
construction — foundations and these roof options alike — falls under the **local building permit**
with site-specific engineering:

- **Texas (TDLR IHB):** the industrialized builder must have a Texas-licensed engineer or architect
  design and seal the foundation "and any unique on-site construction details" — a field-built
  truss roof is exactly that.
- **California (HCD FBH):** the insignia covers the approved factory unit; Design Approval Agency
  scope "may or may not include" stick-built components and roof trusses, and installation is
  inspected by the local building department. Boxabl's own S6 title block states the truss profile
  "has not yet been submitted for approval by the CA Dept. of Housing & Urban Development" but
  "can be used if submitted (along with custom, stand-alone, site-specific engineered Casita
  documents) for local building dept. approval."
- **Other program states** (NV, and the state modular programs generally) follow the same split.

## Who Provides What at Permit Time

| Item | Provided by |
|---|---|
| Roof option concept sheets (S5–S9) | Boxabl, with the unit documentation |
| Site-specific design criteria (wind/exposure/snow/seismic) | Licensed engineer (site engineer) |
| Truss design + sealed shop drawings | Truss manufacturer's engineer, to the criteria above |
| Truss-to-SIP connections, clip spacing, bracing, nailing schedule, orientation determination | Licensed engineer (site engineer) |
| Erection stability plan | Installer / others |
| Roofing material selection within seismic limits | Owner + engineer |
| Local permit submittal | Owner / builder, with the sealed documents above |

## Sample Design Conditions

Reference concepts in public materials often assume criteria such as current IBC/IRC, Risk
Category II, Exposure C, and illustrative wind speeds. **These are educational assumptions only** —
your stamped roof package uses your parcel's values, and your jurisdiction's adopted code governs.
